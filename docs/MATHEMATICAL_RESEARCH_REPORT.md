## Executive Summary

This mathematical research report provides the theoretical derivations, dynamical systems analysis, numerical discretization, network coupling proofs, and arrhythmia verification benchmarks for our cardiac electrophysiology simulation engine.

The project models cardiac excitability using the dimensionless, two-variable **FitzHugh–Nagumo (FHN)** system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), originally derived as a planar reduction of Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley’s four-variable squid giant axon equations. To study spatiotemporal excitation propagation, atrioventricular delay, and cardiac arrhythmias, we extend the isolated single-cell model into a multi-compartment coupled network governed by a graph Laplacian diffusive operator.

## 1. Mathematical Formulation of the Standalone Model (Stage 1)

The dimensionless FitzHugh–Nagumo system is defined by the planar coupled non-linear system:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = f(v, w) = v - \frac{v^3}{3} - w + I$$

$$\frac{dw}{dt} = g(v, w) = \varepsilon(v + a - bw)$$

### 1.1 State Variables and Biophysical Analogues
* **$v(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Fast Voltage-Like Variable):**  
  Represents transmembrane potential. The linear self-excitation term $+v$ models regenerative inward depolarizing current (analogous to voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ or L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents), while the cubic saturation term $-\frac{v^3}{3}$ models rapid self-limiting inactivation.
* **$w(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Slow Recovery Variable):**  
  Represents outward repolarizing currents and refractory deactivation (analogous to delayed-rectifier $\text{K}^+$ currents).
* **$\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$ (Timescale Separation):**  
  A small positive dimensionless parameter ($\varepsilon \ll 1$, typically $\varepsilon \in [0.01, 0.15]$ in cardiac modeling). Because $\frac{dw}{dt} \sim \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$, $w(t)$ evolves on a slow timescale compared to the fast upstroke of $v(t)$, giving rise to the characteristic cardiac action potential plateau and refractory tail.
* **$I \in \mathbb{R}$ (External Applied Current):**  
  Represents transmembrane pacemaker current or continuous intracellular stimulus.
* **$a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ (Nullcline Geometry Parameters):**  
  Constrain the resting potential intercept ($a > 0$) and the slope of the recovery nullcline ($b > 0$, typically $b \in (0, 1)$).

## 2. Nullclines and Equilibrium Analysis

### 2.1 Nullcline Equations
Setting the time derivatives to zero yields the system nullclines:

1. **$v$-nullcline ($\dot{v} = 0$):**
   $$w = v - \frac{v^3}{3} + I$$
   This is a cubic polynomial with local extrema at:
   $$\frac{dw}{dv} = 1 - v^2 = 0 \implies v_{\text{min}} = -1, \quad v_{\text{max}} = +1$$
   - Local Minimum (Knee): $(v, w) = \left(-1, -\frac{2}{3} + I\right)$
   - Local Maximum (Crest): $(v, w) = \left(+1, +\frac{2}{3} + I\right)$

2. **$w$-nullcline ($\dot{w} = 0$):**
   $$w = \frac{v + a}{b} \quad (b \neq 0)$$
   This is a straight line with slope $m = \frac{1}{b}$ and $w$-intercept $w_0 = \frac{a}{b}$.

### 2.2 Analytical Equilibrium Condition (Cardano's Cubic)
Equilibrium points $(v^*, w^*)$ occur at nullcline intersections:

$$v^* - \frac{(v^*)^3}{3} + I = \frac{v^* + a}{b}$$

Multiplying by 3 and rearranging into depressed cubic standard form:

$$(v^*)^3 + 3\left(\frac{1}{b} - 1\right)v^* + 3\left(\frac{a}{b} - I\right) = 0$$

Let:
$$p = 3\left(\frac{1}{b} - 1\right), \quad q = 3\left(\frac{a}{b} - I\right)$$

The cubic discriminant is:
$$\Delta_{\text{cubic}} = \left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{9}{4}\left(\frac{a}{b} - I\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{b} - 1\right)^3$$

#### Theorem 1 (Uniqueness of Equilibrium for $b < 1$):
*If $0 < b < 1$, then $p = 3(\frac{1}{b} - 1) > 0$. Consequently, $(p/3)^3 > 0$, ensuring $\Delta_{\text{cubic}} > 0$ for all real parameters $a, I$. Therefore, the system possesses **exactly one real equilibrium point** for all $I \in \mathbb{R}$.*

Using Cardano’s formula, the unique real root is:
$$v^* = \sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} + \sqrt{\Delta_{\text{cubic}}}} + \sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} - \sqrt{\Delta_{\text{cubic}}}}$$
$$w^* = \frac{v^* + a}{b}$$

In our implementation (`fhn_core/analysis.py`), roots are solved analytically and verified against strict residual thresholds:
$$\|f(v^*, w^*)\|_\infty < 10^{-12}, \quad \|g(v^*, w^*)\|_\infty < 10^{-12}$$

## 3. Linearization, Jacobian Matrix, and Stability

### 3.1 Jacobian Matrix
The Jacobian of the linearized system evaluated at $(v^*, w^*)$ is:

$$J(v^*, w^*) = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} & \frac{\partial f}{\partial w} \\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial v} & \frac{\partial g}{\partial w} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - (v^*)^2 & -1 \\ \varepsilon & -\varepsilon b \end{pmatrix}$$

### 3.2 Trace, Determinant, and Characteristic Equation
The characteristic equation $\det(J - \lambda I) = 0$ is:

$$\lambda^2 - \tau \lambda + \Delta = 0$$

Where:
* **Trace $\tau$:**
  $$\tau = \text{tr}(J) = 1 - (v^*)^2 - \varepsilon b$$
* **Determinant $\Delta$:**
  $$\Delta = \det(J) = -\varepsilon b(1 - (v^*)^2) - (-1)(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon [1 - b(1 - (v^*)^2)]$$
* **Discriminant $D$:**
  $$D = \tau^2 - 4\Delta = [1 - (v^*)^2 - \varepsilon b]^2 - 4\varepsilon [1 - b(1 - (v^*)^2)]$$

The eigenvalues are:
$$\lambda_{1, 2} = \frac{\tau \pm \sqrt{D}}{2}$$

### 3.3 Poincaré Classification Criteria
1. **Saddle Point:** $\Delta < 0$ (eigenvalues are real with opposite signs).
2. **Stable Node:** $\Delta > 0$, $D \ge 0$, $\tau < 0$.
3. **Unstable Node:** $\Delta > 0$, $D \ge 0$, $\tau > 0$.
4. **Stable Focus / Spiral:** $\Delta > 0$, $D < 0$, $\tau < 0$.
5. **Unstable Focus / Spiral:** $\Delta > 0$, $D < 0$, $\tau > 0$.
6. **Center (Neutral):** $\Delta > 0$, $\tau = 0$ (pure imaginary eigenvalues $\lambda = \pm i \sqrt{\Delta}$).

## 4. Bifurcation Analysis

### 4.1 Andronov-Hopf Bifurcation
A Hopf bifurcation occurs when the trace vanishes while the determinant remains positive:

$$\tau(v_H^*) = 0 \iff 1 - (v_H^*)^2 - \varepsilon b = 0 \iff (v_H^*)^2 = 1 - \varepsilon b$$

Since $\varepsilon b > 0$, a necessary condition for real Hopf bifurcation points is:
$$\varepsilon b < 1 \implies v_H^* = \pm \sqrt{1 - \varepsilon b}$$

The critical current $I_H$ at which the Hopf bifurcation occurs is found by inserting $v_H^*$ into the cubic equilibrium relation:
$$I_H = \frac{v_H^* + a}{b} - v_H^* + \frac{(v_H^*)^3}{3}$$

#### Transversality Condition:
$$\left.\frac{d \text{Re}(\lambda)}{dI}\right|_{I = I_H} = \left.\frac{1}{2} \frac{d\tau}{dv^*} \frac{dv^*}{dI}\right|_{I_H} = \left.\frac{1}{2} (-2v_H^*) \left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{b} - 1 + (v_H^*)^2}\right)\right|_{I_H} = -\frac{v_H^*}{\frac{1}{b} - 1 + (v_H^*)^2} \neq 0$$

Because the real part of the eigenvalues crosses zero with non-zero speed, the Hopf bifurcation is transversal. For $I \in (I_{H1}, I_{H2})$, the fixed point is an unstable focus surrounded by a **stable limit cycle**, producing self-sustained periodic action potential oscillations (pacemaking).

### 4.2 Non-Existence of Fold (Saddle-Node) Bifurcations for Cardiac $b \in (0, 1)$
A fold bifurcation requires $\Delta = 0$. In our system:
$$\Delta = \varepsilon [1 - b(1 - (v^*)^2)] = 0 \implies 1 - (v^*)^2 = \frac{1}{b} \implies (v^*)^2 = 1 - \frac{1}{b}$$

For $0 < b < 1$:
$$\frac{1}{b} > 1 \implies 1 - \frac{1}{b} < 0$$
Because $(v^*)^2 \ge 0$ for all real states, no real equilibrium can satisfy $(v^*)^2 = 1 - 1/b$.  
**Conclusion:** Saddle-node bifurcations cannot occur in standard cardiac parameter regimes ($b \in (0, 1)$); bistability of equilibria is rigorously excluded.

## 5. Coupled Conduction Network Formulation (Stage 2)

### 5.1 Multi-Compartment Differential Equations
To simulate cardiac tissue conduction without fictitious delay timers, we discretize the myocardium into an interconnected network of $N$ discrete compartments:

$$\frac{dv_i}{dt} = v_i - \frac{v_i^3}{3} - w_i + I_i(t) + D \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} A_{ij}(v_j - v_i)$$

$$\frac{dw_i}{dt} = \varepsilon_i(v_i + a_i - b_i w_i)$$

Where:
- $i, j \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$ index anatomical compartments.
- $A_{ij} \ge 0$ is the symmetric or directed adjacency coupling weight (gap-junction conductance).
- $D > 0$ is the global diffusion coefficient.
- $\mathcal{N}(i)$ denotes the set of cells coupled to compartment $i$.

In vector notation:
$$\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}) + \mathbf{I}(t) - D \mathbf{L} \mathbf{v}$$
$$\frac{d\mathbf{w}}{dt} = \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w})$$
Where $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{D}_{\text{deg}} - \mathbf{A}$ is the graph Laplacian matrix, satisfying positive semi-definiteness $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{L} \mathbf{x} \ge 0$.

### 5.2 Six-Compartment Anatomical Reference Network
Our reference conduction network models the primary electrical axis:

| Node ID | Anatomical Compartment | $\varepsilon_i$ | Intrinsic Role | Coupling $g_{ij}$ |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|:---:|
| **0** | Sinoatrial (SA) Node | 0.08 | Primary Pacemaker | $g_{01} = 0.35$ |
| **1** | Atrial Region (RA/LA) | 0.08 | Fast Atrial Spread | $g_{12} = 0.35$ (Configurable) |
| **2** | Atrioventricular (AV) Node | 0.08 | Conduction Bottleneck / Physiological Delay | $g_{23} = 0.35$ (Configurable) |
| **3** | Bundle of His | 0.08 | Septal Conduction Highway | $g_{34} = 0.40$ |
| **4** | Purkinje Arborization | 0.08 | Rapid Ventricular Distribution | $g_{45} = 0.50$ |
| **5** | Ventricular Myocardium | 0.08 | Ventricular Systole Depolarization | Output Region |

### 5.3 Emergent Wave Speed and Gap-Junction Delay
In the continuum limit $\Delta x \to 0$, the discrete network converges to the reaction-diffusion PDE:
$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} = v - \frac{v^3}{3} - w + I + D \frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial x^2}$$

The asymptotic wave speed $c$ of the depolarizing wavefront satisfies the scaling law:
$$c \propto \sqrt{D}$$

Conduction delay across a pathway of length $\Delta x$ is therefore purely emergent:
$$\Delta t_{\text{propagation}} = \int \frac{dx}{c(x)}$$

In accordance with Mines (1913), Garrey (1914), and Allessie (1973), a re-entrant excitation wave can only persist around an obstacle of perimeter $L$ if the circuit transit time exceeds the effective refractory period (ERP):

$$T_{\text{loop}} = \oint \frac{ds}{c} > \text{ERP}$$

If $T_{\text{loop}} < \text{ERP}$, the wave head encounters the refractory tail ($w_i > -0.35$) and extinguishes immediately.

#### Honest Numerical Benchmark:
- **10-Node Ring ($g=0.35, \varepsilon=0.15$):**  
  Conduction delay around loop is $T_{\text{loop}} \approx 23.91$ units.

  Since $23.91 > 16.0$, the wave successfully re-excites Node 0, sustaining **6 spontaneous revolutions** without further external stimulus.
- **4-Node Ring ($g=0.35, \varepsilon=0.15$):**  
  Conduction delay is $T_{\text{loop}} \approx 5.2$ units $< \text{ERP}$.  
  The wave collides with its own refractory tail at Node 2 and extinguishes after 0.5 revolutions. The software faithfully reports `reentry_sustained = False`.

## 7. Pseudo-ECG Waveform Synthesis via Dipole Projection

Because a multi-compartment discrete network does not inhabit a continuous volume conductor with boundary conditions, surface 12-lead ECGs cannot be calculated directly. Instead, we project the network's instantaneous intercellular current dipoles along the vertical base-to-apex cardiac axis $\vec{u}_y$:

$$\Phi(t) = \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} g_{ij} [v_j(t) - v_i(t)] \cdot (\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i)_y$$

- Atrial activation produces a distinct initial deflection (analogous to the P wave).
- AV conduction delay produces an isoelectric segment (analogous to the PR interval).
- Purkinje-to-ventricle activation produces a sharp high-amplitude biphasic deflection (analogous to the QRS complex).
- Ventricular recovery produces a slower deflection (analogous to the T wave).

## 9. Rhythm Visualization & Activation Analysis (Stage 5)

### 9.1 Multi-Lead Dipole Field Formulation
In addition to the longitudinal dipole trace $\Phi(t)$, our engine implements a generalized far-field Einthoven limb lead projection:
$$\vec{P}(t) = \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} g_{ij} [v_j(t) - v_i(t)] (\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i)$$
Projected onto Einthoven Lead II ($60^\circ$ vector from right shoulder to left lower abdomen):
$$V_{\text{Lead II}}(t) = \vec{P}(t) \cdot \begin{pmatrix} \cos 60^\circ \\ \sin 60^\circ \end{pmatrix} = 0.5 P_x(t) + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} P_y(t)$$

#### Physical Source-to-Electrode Assumptions:
1. **Far-Field Dipole Approximation:** Assumes cardiac dimensions are small compared to boundary distances.
2. **Homogeneous Isotropic Volume Conductor:** Neglects electrical conductivity jumps across blood ($0.6\text{ S/m}$), myocardium ($0.2\text{ S/m}$), lungs ($0.05\text{ S/m}$), and ribs.
3. **Quasi-Static Assumption:** Neglects displacement currents (valid for frequencies $< 1\text{ kHz}$).
4. **Non-Diagnostic:** Educational surrogate only; not a clinical 12-lead diagnostic ECG.

### 9.2 Beat-to-Beat Interval Sequences & Timescale Calibration
Let $\{t_k\}_{k=1}^K$ denote the sequence of upward threshold crossings ($v_k > 0$) for a monitored chamber. The beat-to-beat cycle length sequence is:
$$\Delta t_k = t_{k+1} - t_k, \quad k = 1, \dots, K-1$$

Mean cycle length and variability (SDNN analogue) are:
$$\overline{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{K-1} \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} \Delta t_k, \quad \sigma_{\Delta t} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K-2} \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} (\Delta t_k - \overline{\Delta t})^2}$$

#### Dimensionless-to-Physical Rate Calibration:
In standard human electrophysiology, resting cycle length at $\approx 67\text{ BPM}$ is $\approx 900\text{ ms}$. In our calibrated FHN model ($a=0.7, b=0.8, \varepsilon=0.08$), normal pacing interval is $T_{\text{pace}} = 45.0$ model time units. This establishes the physical calibration factor:
$$\tau_{\text{scale}} = 0.020\text{ s/unit} \quad (1\text{ model time unit} \approx 20\text{ ms})$$
$$\text{Estimated Heart Rate (BPM)} = \frac{60}{\overline{\Delta t} \times \tau_{\text{scale}}}$$
If fewer than 2 ventricular activations occur (e.g. Complete AV Block), the software reports $\text{BPM} = 0.0$ (Asystole).

### 9.3 Algorithmic Missing Event Detection
- **Sinus Pause:** Expected pacing pulses $\{k \cdot T_{\text{pace}}\}$ falling within $[t_{\text{pause\_start}}, t_{\text{pause\_end}}]$ are identified. When no threshold crossing occurs within $\pm 5.0$ units, the impulse is logged as a suppressed sinus event.
- **AV Conduction Block:** Each atrial activation $t_{\text{atr}}$ is paired with downstream ventricular activations within a physiological conduction window ($[t_{\text{atr}}, t_{\text{atr}} + 28.0]$). If no ventricular activation exists, the event is logged as a dropped downstream beat.

### 9.4 Numerical Activation Event Logging
Sub-step linear interpolation at the upward threshold crossing ($v=0.0$) logs exact timestamps:
$$t^* = t_{k-1} + \frac{0.0 - v_{k-1}}{v_k - v_{k-1}} \Delta t$$
Every event across all 6 compartments records: `Simulation Time`, `Region`, `Node ID`, `Threshold Crossing Voltage`, `Previous Activation Time`, `Interval Since Previous`, and `Conduction Status`.

## 10. Mathematical Analysis Laboratory (Stage 6)

### 10.1 Academic Scope & Planar Boundary
The mathematical laboratory focuses exclusively on the two-dimensional single-cell FitzHugh–Nagumo dynamical system:
$$\frac{dv}{dt} = f(v, w) = v - \frac{v^3}{3} - w + I$$
$$\frac{dw}{dt} = g(v, w) = \varepsilon(v + a - bw)$$

**Critical Architectural Separation:**  
The two-dimensional phase plane, nullclines, and planar Poincaré stability classifications apply strictly to the standalone single-cell system ($\mathbb{R}^2$). They must **not** be confused with or applied to the $2N$-dimensional state space ($\mathbb{R}^{2N}$) of the coupled cardiac network.

### 10.2 Part A & B: Nullclines, Cardano Equilibrium Roots & Residual Verification
1. **$v$-nullcline ($\dot{v} = 0$):**
   $$w = v - \frac{v^3}{3} + I$$
2. **$w$-nullcline ($\dot{w} = 0$):**
   $$w = \frac{v + a}{b}$$
3. **Cardano Cubic Derivation:**  
   Intersection points satisfy $b v^3 + 3(1 - b)v + 3(a - bI) = 0$. For $b \in (0, 1)$, the linear coefficient $3(1-b) > 0$, guaranteeing a strictly positive cubic discriminant $\Delta_{\text{cubic}} > 0$ and exactly one real equilibrium root $(v^*, w^*)$.
4. **Numerical Residual Verification:**  
   Every computed equilibrium is validated against machine-precision residuals:
   $$\|r_v\| = \left| v^* - \frac{(v^*)^3}{3} - w^* + I \right| < 10^{-12}, \quad \|r_w\| = |\varepsilon(v^* + a - b w^*)| < 10^{-12}$$

### 10.3 Part C: Analytical Jacobian & Poincaré Stability
Linearization around an equilibrium $(v^*, w^*)$ yields the analytical Jacobian matrix:
$$J(v^*, w^*) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - (v^*)^2 & -1 \\ \varepsilon & -\varepsilon b \end{pmatrix}$$

The linear stability invariants are:
- **Trace:** $\tau = \operatorname{tr}(J) = 1 - (v^*)^2 - \varepsilon b$
- **Determinant:** $\Delta = \det(J) = \varepsilon [1 - b + b (v^*)^2]$
- **Discriminant:** $D = \tau^2 - 4\Delta$
- **Eigenvalues:** $\lambda_{1,2} = \frac{\tau \pm \sqrt{D}}{2}$

**Poincaré Classification Scheme:**
- $\Delta < 0$: Saddle Point (Hyperbolic, Unstable).
- $\Delta > 0, D \ge 0, \tau < 0$: Stable Node (Monotonic Exponential Sink).
- $\Delta > 0, D \ge 0, \tau > 0$: Unstable Node (Monotonic Exponential Source).
- $\Delta > 0, D < 0, \tau < 0$: Locally Asymptotically Stable Focus (Damped Spiral Sink).
- $\Delta > 0, D < 0, \tau > 0$: Unstable Focus (Spiral Source / Onset of Stable Limit Cycle).
- $\tau = 0, \Delta > 0$: Non-Hyperbolic Center / Andronov-Hopf Boundary.
- $\Delta = 0$: Non-Hyperbolic Fold / Saddle-Node Boundary.

### 10.4 Part D: Phase-Plane Flow Fields & Normalized Direction Vectors
In the $(v, w)$ phase plane, fast-slow dynamics cause large disparities in velocity magnitudes ($|\dot{v}| \sim 1$ while $|\dot{w}| \sim \varepsilon \ll 1$). Direction fields are rendered using normalized direction vectors:
$$\hat{f}(v, w) = \frac{\dot{v}}{\sqrt{\dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2 + \delta}}, \quad \hat{g}(v, w) = \frac{\dot{w}}{\sqrt{\dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2 + \delta}}$$
Trajectories integrated via 4th-order Runge-Kutta from selectable initial coordinates $(v_0, w_0)$ confirm convergence to either the stable spiral fixed point (resting quiescence) or the enclosed periodic limit cycle (pacemaker autorhythmicity).

### 10.5 Part E: Multi-Parameter Bifurcation Sweeps ($I, \varepsilon, a, b$)
1. **Andronov-Hopf Bifurcation:**  
   Occurs where $\tau = 0$ with $\Delta > 0$. The critical voltage is $(v_H^*)^2 = 1 - \varepsilon b$. The critical applied current is:
   $$I_{\text{Hopf}} = \frac{v_H^* + a}{b} - v_H^* + \frac{(v_H^*)^3}{3}$$
   The transversality condition $\left.\frac{d\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)}{dI}\right|_{I_H} \neq 0$ is verified numerically.
2. **Theorem 2 (Fold Non-Existence for Physiological $b < 1$):**  
   A saddle-node fold bifurcation requires $\Delta = \varepsilon[1 - b + b(v^*)^2] = 0 \iff (v^*)^2 = 1 - 1/b$. For physiological cardiac tissue $b \in (0, 1)$, $1 - 1/b < 0$. Because $(v^*)^2 \ge 0$ for all real states, no real equilibrium can satisfy $\Delta = 0$. Multiple equilibria and fold bifurcations can only occur when $b > 1.0$.
3. **Node-Focus Boundary:**  
   Separates oscillatory spiral relaxation from monotonic exponential decay along the parabola $D = \tau^2 - 4\Delta = 0$.

### 10.6 Part F: Parameter Comparison Laboratory
A dedicated before-and-after state comparison engine compares Baseline State A against Perturbed State B:
- Computes parameter deltas: $\Delta I, \Delta \varepsilon, \Delta a, \Delta b$.
- Evaluates dual Jacobian metrics and eigenvalues.
- Diagnoses the topological regime transition:
  - *Restabilization:* Unstable focus (limit cycle) $\to$ stable quiescent fixed point.
  - *Hopf Excitation:* Quiescent focus $\to$ unstable focus with autorhythmic limit cycle firing.
  - *Damping Mode Shift:* Stable focus $\to$ stable node without loss of asymptotic stability.
  - *Equilibrium Translation:* Voltage coordinate shift maintaining invariant topological stability.

- **Parametric Delta Tracking:** Computes exact differences $\Delta \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{trial}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{baseline}}$.
- **Electrophysiological Transition Synthesis:** Automatically characterizes whether parameter alterations produced conduction acceleration, decremental delay, conduction block (dropped ventricular depolarizations), or circus movement re-entry.
- **Waveform Overlay:** Displays synchronized side-by-side or overlaid traces ($\Phi_{\text{base}}(t)$ in blue vs $\Phi_{\text{trial}}(t)$ in amber) to visualize activation timing shifts directly.
